E-max Veneers Explained: What You Need to Know

Why the material matters more than the starting price, and what "E-max" actually means.

LuminedentPublished 4 September 2026Updated 8 September 20266 min read

If you're considering veneers, you've probably already seen wildly different prices — anywhere from £150 to over £750 per tooth. The difference is almost never about the dentist. It's about the material.

Not all veneers are equal

There are two main categories of veneers: composite (resin) and ceramic, like E-max. Both get called "veneers", but the result and lifespan are very different.

  • Composite: cheaper, but stains over time, loses its shine and chips more easily. Average lifespan: 5-7 years.
  • E-max (lithium-disilicate ceramic): doesn't stain, reflects light like natural enamel, more durable. Average lifespan: 15-20 years.

Why we only work with E-max

We could offer a cheaper composite option. We don't, for a simple reason: veneers are about your smile, the most visible part of your face. It's not the trade-off we think is right to offer a patient, even if it would lower the starting price on paper.

The €300-per-tooth price therefore always reflects the same quality — there's no "budget" version you discover only after booking.

How many veneers you need for a full smile

Most patients choose between 6 and 8 veneers for the upper arch — the teeth visible when you smile. The exact number depends on the shape of your mouth and is confirmed during digital design, before any procedure.

  • 6-8 veneers (upper arch): from €1,800
  • 16 veneers (full arch): €4,800

What happens to your natural tooth

To fit a veneer, a very thin layer of enamel is removed — less than a millimetre in most cases — to ensure a precise fit. Your natural tooth stays protected under the ceramic for the lifespan of the veneer.

Before we start, we design your new smile digitally: you see a realistic preview of the result before any procedure, so you know exactly what to expect when you arrive at the clinic.

How long it takes

In most cases the whole treatment is completed in a single 5-7 day trip: preparation and digital scanning on days 1-2, temporary veneers while your final ones are made, final fitting on day 4-6.

The bottom line

If a veneer quote seems surprisingly low, it's worth explicitly asking what material is being used. The difference between composite and E-max isn't a minor technical detail — it's the difference between a result that lasts 5 years and one that lasts 15-20.

What a full smile costs

A per-tooth price says little until you multiply it. These are the real numbers on the combinations people actually ask for.

  • 6-8 veneers, upper arch: from €1,800 — the teeth that show when you smile
  • Smile design, 8 veneers: €2,400, against £3,600-6,000 in the UK
  • 16 veneers, full arch: €4,800

The exact number isn't decided by you in front of a mirror, and not by us from a price list: it comes out of the digital design, where you can see how many teeth actually fall inside your smile line.

How the smile is designed before anything starts

Photos, not an X-ray

For veneers the starting point is a few photos of your smile, not a panoramic X-ray. You send them on WhatsApp or through the form, and the confirmation with the exact price and available dates comes back within 2 hours.

The digital design

Before a tooth is touched, the new smile is designed digitally: shape, length, proportion to your face. It exists so the decision is made together — and so you are told beforehand if what you have in mind is achievable on your teeth.

One trip

Unlike implants, veneers are usually completed in a single trip: preparation, temporary veneers and fitting of the finals within the same stay.

Pain, sensitivity and upkeep

Preparation is done under local anaesthetic, so you don't feel pain during it. In the days after, mild sensitivity to hot and cold is normal and settles on its own.

After that, upkeep is the same as for natural teeth — brushing, floss, regular professional cleaning — with one addition: no nails, pens or bottle caps opened with your teeth, and if you grind at night you need a nightguard. Ceramic is resistant, not indestructible.

Veneers or a crown? They are not the same thing

This is the most common confusion, and it is expensive to get wrong.

A veneer covers the surface

It covers only the visible face of the tooth, removing less than a millimetre of enamel. It makes sense when the tooth is sound and the problem is aesthetic: colour that no longer responds to whitening, irregular shape, small gaps, chipped edges.

A crown wraps the whole tooth

It encloses the tooth entirely, and it is the right answer when the tooth is compromised: heavily restored, root-treated or fractured. It costs €180 per tooth in metal-free zirconia, and sometimes it is the correct answer even if you came in thinking about veneers.

Which one you need isn't decided from photos on a website: it is decided on your case, and the honest answer is sometimes the cheaper of the two.

When veneers should wait

Veneers go on a healthy mouth. Active decay, inflamed gums or an ongoing infection are treated first: these show up in the initial assessment, and no serious clinic skips them to get to the aesthetic result sooner.

If you grind your teeth at night, that is managed alongside the treatment, usually with a nightguard: ceramic is strong, but no material takes years of unprotected night-time load. This too is said beforehand, not after the first chip.

What to ask before saying yes

  1. Which material do you use, and can I have it in writing in the treatment plan?
  2. Do I see the digital design before any tooth is touched?
  3. How many teeth does my case actually need, and why that number?
  4. Does the price include the design, the temporaries and the check-ups?
  5. What does the guarantee cover if a veneer chips?

Five questions, and all five answers should arrive before you book the flight.

Frequently asked questions

How long do E-max veneers last?

15-20 years on average with good oral hygiene, compared with 5-7 years for composite veneers, which stain, lose their shine and chip more easily.

How many veneers do I need for a full smile?

Most patients choose 6-8 veneers for the upper arch — the teeth that show when you smile — from €1,800. A full arch of 16 veneers costs €4,800. The exact number is set during the digital design, before any treatment starts.

Do veneers damage the natural tooth?

A very thin layer of enamel is removed — less than a millimetre in most cases — so the veneer bonds properly. The natural tooth stays protected under the ceramic for as long as the veneer lasts.
